Policy-gradient experiment assumptions

A record of the choices behind the additional comparisons, how outcomes were evaluated, and safeguards against reward hacking.

Purpose. These runs compare training procedures with the repository’s existing game and exact finite-horizon evaluator. They do not establish convergence, a new algorithmic guarantee, or publication readiness. Values below are exploratory evidence and should not be presented as confirmatory results.

Reward and reporting safeguards

  • The game reward and transition rules stay fixed within each comparison. No reward shaping, win-rate bonus, or altered terminal reward was introduced to raise the reported score.
  • Training entropy is a separate optimization setting. The default coefficient is 0.01; a 0.05 run is reported as an explicit ablation. It changes the policy objective’s regularization term, not the soccer payoff or evaluator.
  • These are exploratory runs, not a confirmatory sweep. Report every tested setting and seed; before a new comparison, freeze the game, reward, hyperparameters, seeds, and primary metric, then evaluate on fresh seeds. Win rate is diagnostic only: the exact best response optimizes discounted score difference, not win probability.

Training and evaluation assumptions

  • The earlier PG benchmark uses seeds 0–2. The new matched A10 pilot uses seeds 4–6, 2,000 iterations, 64 episodes per batch, five gradient passes per collected batch, and entropy coefficient 0.01. Its standard A2C context arm uses the usual 10-step bootstrapped advantage. The learned-critic MC control and frozen exact-value critic both use sampled full returns for the actor; only the state-value baseline training differs. Seed IDs do not create common random-number trajectories. Players have independent policy/value networks and no imposed mirror symmetry.
  • Policies are snapshotted every five iterations. Training samples an opponent snapshot uniformly; the reported policy averages its action probabilities state by state. Greedy snapshot selection and network-weight averaging are separate ablations, not interchangeable forms of fictitious play.
  • Each seat is evaluated in 1,000 kickoff games. Exact finite-horizon best responses produce exploitability in discounted-value units; the best response optimizes that value, not win chance. Win/tie/loss against random, exact Nash, and exact best response are separate sampled frequencies.
  • The frozen-value experiment fits each critic to exact state-time values once, freezes it, and updates the actor with sampled discounted returns minus that baseline. It does not query exact action values during actor updates. The separate a2c_exact method uses oracle advantages and is a different experiment. The matched a2c_mc control learns its baseline from the same sampled full returns used by the actor estimator.

Interpretation and next steps

Limits and next comparison

  • None of these learned policies matches the exact equilibrium. In the deterministic A10 runs, wins against the value-best response stay near zero. The random move-order results also fall below the exact solver’s seat-specific baseline in important matchups.
  • In the seeds 4–6 matched pilot, the frozen-minus-learned-MC Nash-gap differences were +0.066, −0.041, and +0.015 (mean +0.013). This sample is too small and inconsistent to support a reliable benefit or harm claim.
  • The entropy ablation is exploratory and was inspected against the same evaluation metrics. Seed 3 repeats the lower exploitability but not meaningful best-response wins. It must remain labeled an ablation; the current evidence is not an unbiased test of a preselected method.
  • Before making a research claim, fix a reward and evaluator, specify both game formats, preselect one primary value-based metric and the seat aggregation, use fresh seeds, report uncertainty, and publish all configurations and failures.
  • The directly related soccer study, Competitive Policy Optimization (UAI 2021), evaluates a different game/reward and a different algorithm. Its results motivate future algorithmic comparisons; they do not validate these runs.
